# §3 — provisional English translation

**Source status:** Follows GS V (Leitzmann 1906), §3, printed365–366 / PDF375–376 / scan leaves377–378. Drechsel (2024:155) reports cancellation of the first seven paragraphs. Manuscript marks, extent and editorial meaning remain independently unverified. Retained as the printed reading; authorial finality unresolved.

**Review status:** Brett completed English-readability review (“very natural”). This is a model-assisted research translation, not independently certified German fidelity.

It may therefore well be said, with good reason, that the general study of language calls for considering the American languages in their interconnection, inquiring into and describing their structure, and comparing it with that of the languages of the other continents. My intention in the present work is to do this to the best of my abilities, with due completeness, but above all through inquiry that penetrates as deeply as possible into the essential character of these languages. To achieve this aim, I have for a number of years not merely studied the individual languages but also prepared my own treatments of them, and for an even longer time collected materials, chiefly in manuscript. Collecting of this kind can never attain completeness; that is inherent in the nature of the matter itself. But the resources that are at my disposal and are to be regarded as sources for this investigation provide, when combined with what has already been very usefully prepared in several other works, abundant material for a treatment covering the whole field of this investigation, and sufficient material for a reliable assessment.

## Translator’s notes

1. Interconnection describes the comparative relations considered here; it does not establish one original American language. “As a connected whole” remains possible. “Continents” follows the earlier paragraphs’ local treatment of Welttheile.

2. “Essential character” renders Wesen locally and distinguishes it from Natur in sentence4. “Essence” is an alternative; no single English equivalent is imposed on all occurrences of Wesen.

3. “Prepared my own treatments” retains the contrast between studirt and bearbeitet without specifying completed grammars, dictionaries or fieldwork. The force of selbst may also be scalar (“even”). “Chiefly in manuscript” describes the materials, not necessarily Humboldt’s own handwriting.

4. The final sentence repeats “material” for the German ellipsis and preserves abundant material for comprehensive treatment versus sufficient material for reliable assessment. The earlier preparatory work combines with the resources in providing material; it is not silently identified with the source collection.
