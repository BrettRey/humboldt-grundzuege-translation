## claude — claude-fable-5-2

It may therefore well be called, with justice, a need of the general study of language to consider the American languages in their interconnection, to investigate and set forth their structure, and to compare it with the structure of the languages of the other parts of the world. To do this according to my powers, with due completeness, but above all with an investigation that penetrates as deeply as possible into the essential nature of these languages, is my intention in the present work. In order to achieve this intention, I have for a number of years not merely studied the individual languages but worked them up myself, and for a still longer time I have collected materials, chiefly manuscript ones. That collecting of this kind can never attain completeness lies in the very nature of the matter. But the resources that are at my disposal, and that are to be regarded as the sources for this investigation, offer, when combined with the preparatory work already very serviceably done in several other works, abundant material for a treatment embracing the whole field of this investigation, and sufficient material for a secure judgment.
