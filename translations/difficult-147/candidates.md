## claude: claude-fable-5-2

**s01:** Here, however, one circumstance must not be left out of account, a circumstance which does indeed weaken the disadvantage arising from the lack of genuine grammatical forms in languages, and which could be regarded as a victorious objection to what has just been said.

**s02:** Even if a grammatical form possesses no designation in the language, it is nevertheless present, as a guiding law of understanding, in those who speak the language.

**s03:** At the same time it is also, in whatever manner this may be, indicated in some way in the language itself, and it can therefore even seem doubtful whether it is to be ascribed to the language or denied to it, since by language one understands, after all, not merely the dead sounds but the apprehension of them by those who speak.

**s04:** It remains no less necessary on that account, however, to distinguish this apprehension precisely from the dead sounds, and to speak here solely of what actually lies in the sensible sound.

**s05:** By grammatical form I therefore understand here an indication of the grammatical relation, in the audible part of the language itself, that is so fixed and pure that the grammatical relation indicated is determinately recognized in it, and that the manner of indication cannot be taken for a designation of a concept at all, but immediately, through itself, makes manifest the difference between the designation of concepts and the designation of relations.

**s06:** On the manner of indication I can, in keeping with the order deliberately chosen (134.), explain myself only in what follows; here I do not exclude even indication by word order, provided that it is absolutely fixed, immovable, and related to a fixed point that is not itself in turn recognizable only by its position.

**s07:** If I now assume the case that such an indication of a grammatical form as the one just described is entirely lacking in a language, or is effected not formally but by means of a material concept [Sachbegriff], the form is nonetheless carried into the language all the same, and thus also acts back again from the language upon the soul.

**s08:** Thus in Chinese, for example, there is no designation of the verb as a grammatical form.

**s09:** But the language possesses words which, although grammatically they could also be nouns, are always taken in the language only as verbs.

**s10:** The Chinese speaker therefore never sees in such a word anything other than a verb, and for him the language accordingly has verbs, provided only that one does not attend to whether the verbal nature lies in a grammatical designation or in the habit of linguistic usage.

**s11:** The same is found where a word in Chinese can be used as a noun just as well as it can as a verb, but the connection of the sense, or the position, indicates that the latter is to be the case.

**s12:** Yet position in Chinese is not an indication of grammatical form in the sense just established, if only because it lacks a fixed centre outside itself.

**s13:** It happens further in languages that what were originally material words [Sachwörter] pass over into real relational words [Verhältnisswörter].

**s14:** In Chinese, ì is a verb and has the meaning of to use, to apply.

**s15:** But it also serves as a preposition, and Mr Abel-Rémusat observes very rightly that the nation which often has the word on its lips in this latter meaning gradually forgets the original verbal sense entirely.

**s16:** Here, admittedly, this forgetting becomes more difficult, since the word still has currency as a verb even today.

**s17:** Yet so often the case is such that the original material meaning has perished entirely and can scarcely still be recognized etymologically, and then such a grammatical word acts merely as a relational concept.

**s18:** This, however, can have come about only little by little, in gradual transition, and so it is no longer always possible to tell from the language alone what is taken as truly grammatical, as a genuine and pure designation of relation; for that, one would have to know the nation's mode of apprehension, and know it in different epochs.
