**Scope:** Title, subtitle, and the first complete paragraph. The German below was checked against the scan. The English is a draft for review. Translator’s notes follow separately.

## Draft English

### Outlines of the General Type of Language

*As an Introduction to Detailed Investigations of the American Languages*

[1.] Although it may seem odd to make the languages of a single continent the subject of an inquiry, this approach, which at first sight is purely geographical, is justified in the case of America by the continent’s isolated position and the distinctive character of its languages. In Europe, Asia, and Africa, such diverse languages have come together that linguistic scholarship, at least, can find no reason to study them in the particular grouping in which, always more or less by chance, they now find themselves within the boundaries of their respective continents. The language stocks of each also vary greatly, both in their structure and in the stages of development they have reached. Apart from the most recent European immigration, whose only effect on this continent’s inner national character has been destructive, America, by contrast, has experienced either no immigration at all or only immigration of which scarcely a trace that could be called historical remains. Whatever the origin of the American peoples and languages may be, therefore, their fortunes have been shaped over many centuries only by themselves and by contact with one another. For as long as we know the history of this vast continent, and down to European subjugation, its innumerable tribes, hordes, and families, from the farthest north to the farthest south, were free of foreign influence: their powers were set fermenting solely from within, and they shared only their own distinctive characteristics with one another. Whatever foreign elements may have been introduced in still earlier times have therefore subsequently undergone, for centuries, this smoothing effect of self-contained interaction among the native populations.

## Checked German

### Grundzüge des allgemeinen Sprachtypus.

*Als Einleitung zu ausführlichen Untersuchungen über die Amerikanischen Sprachen.*

[1.] Wenn es vielleicht sonderbar scheint, gerade die Sprachen eines Welttheils zum Gegenstande einer Untersuchung zu machen, so rechtfertigt sich diese, dem ersten Anblick nach, bloss geographische Ansicht bei Amerika durch die abgesonderte Lage dieses Welttheils und die besondre Beschaffenheit seiner Sprachen. In Europa, Asien und Afrika sind so verschiedene Sprachen zusammengeflossen, dass wenigstens die Sprachkunde keine Veranlassung finden kann, dieselben gerade in der Verbindung zu untersuchen, in die sie, immer mehr oder weniger zufällig, nun eben innerhalb der Gränzen desselben Welttheils getreten sind. Auch zeigen die Sprachstämme eines jeden die bedeutendsten Verschiedenheiten sowohl in ihrem Bau, als in den Stufen der von ihnen erreichten Bildung. Amerika dagegen hat, ausser den neuesten Europäischen Einwanderungen, welche die innere Nationaleigenthümlichkeit dieses Welttheils nur durch ihren zerstörenden Einfluss berührt haben, entweder gar keine, oder nur solche erfahren, von welchen kaum eine geschichtlich zu nennende Spur übriggeblieben ist. Welches daher auch der Ursprung der Amerikanischen Völker und Sprachen seyn mag, so haben sich ihre Geschicke viele Jahrhunderte hindurch nur durch sich selbst, und durch Berührung unter einander gestaltet. Die unübersehbare Menge von Stämmen, Horden und Familien vom obersten Norden bis zum untersten Süden des ausgedehnten Welttheils sind, solange wir die Geschichte desselben kennen, bis auf die Europaeische Unterjochung frei von fremdem Einfluss, nur durch sich selbst in Gährung ihrer Kräfte gerathen, und haben sich nur ihre Eigenthümlichkeiten mitgetheilt. Was daher auch in noch früherer Zeit Fremdes eingebracht seyn möchte, hat nachher Jahrhunderte hindurch diesen abschleifenden Einfluss des auf sich selbst beschränkten einheimischen Verkehrs erfahren.

## Translator’s notes

These choices apply to this pilot and remain provisional.

| German | Draft rendering | Reason for the choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *allgemeiner Sprachtypus* | general type of language | Keeps *Typus* as “type”; the title remains open for review. |
| *Welttheil* | continent | “America” here covers the continent from its far north to its far south. |
| *Sprachkunde* | linguistic scholarship | Names the study of languages without fixing a modern disciplinary label. |
| *Sprachstämme* | language stocks | Keeps this distinct from *Stämme, Horden und Familien*, the human groupings later in the paragraph. |
| *Bau* | structure | A candidate recurring equivalent, to be checked in later passages. |
| *Bildung* | development | In this sentence, languages attain stages of *Bildung*; the choice is local to that context. |
| *Nationaleigenthümlichkeit* | national character | “Inner” preserves *innere*. The phrase’s scope and English fluency need review. |
| *Gährung ihrer Kräfte* | their powers were set fermenting | Retains Humboldt’s fermentation metaphor. |
| *einheimischer Verkehr* | interaction among the native populations | Takes *Verkehr* as interaction in the paragraph’s account of contact among peoples. |

The final sentence’s “smoothing effect” renders *abschleifenden Einfluss*. “Levelling effect” is another candidate, but would make the reduction of differences more explicit. The scope of *Fremdes* is left broad in “foreign elements.”

## Transcription and review limits

The original paragraph is preserved as one paragraph in each language. Spelling, capitalization, and punctuation follow the 1906 printing in the German; line breaks and line-end hyphens are removed. The page break divides *Ameri-kanischen* in the fifth sentence. The bracketed section number is retained; marginal placement and letterspacing are not reproduced.

The small-print source note at the foot of printed p. 364 belongs to the edition’s apparatus and is excluded here. This pilot has not been collated against Humboldt’s manuscript or another edition. Historical group labels and evaluative claims remain part of Humboldt’s text.

For review, focus on the title, the balance between readable English and Humboldt’s sentence structure, and the provisional terms above. This draft covers no part of §2.
