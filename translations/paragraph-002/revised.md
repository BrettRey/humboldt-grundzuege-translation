> **Provisional source status:** Follows GS V (Leitzmann 1906), §2, printed p. 365 / PDF p. 375. Drechsel (2024:155) reports cancellation of the first seven paragraphs. Manuscript marks, extent and editorial meaning have not been independently verified. Retained as the printed reading; authorial finality unresolved.

Further support for this comes from the striking similarity of the American languages. There have certainly been substantial differences in the development of the nations to which they belong, differences unmistakable in the languages themselves as well; yet the lack of alphabetic writing, and hence of literature in the proper sense, places them all, without exception, on an equal footing at a lower level. It is true that the sometimes wrongly asserted sameness of their structure disappears under more penetrating investigation, as does their deviation from the structure of all other languages, which has also been asserted. Nevertheless, all the languages of America do in fact exhibit broad features of similarity, and the north and south of the continent do not permit genuinely separating boundaries to be drawn even in this respect.

## Editorial notes

- **References:** “this” continues §1’s argument. “In the languages themselves” is an interpretation of *in ihnen selbst*; the nations themselves are another possible antecedent. “Them all” leaves *alle* pronominal, though the explicit earlier choice may lead an English reader toward languages. “Nations” retains Humboldt’s wording without specifying modern nation-states.
- **Evaluation:** alphabetic writing, literature “in the proper sense,” and the “lower level” are Humboldt’s qualifications and judgment. The translation preserves them; it does not turn “alphabetic” into a claim about all writing. The parallel fragment lacks the explicit lower-level phrase.
- **Similarity and sameness:** the paragraph rejects alleged sameness of structure and alleged deviation from all other languages, while retaining similarity. “Also been asserted” leaves the nuance of *wohl* open. “Even in this respect” preserves *darin* without claiming a uniquely settled referent. English plural agreement is idiomatic; the checked German retains singular *erlaubt*.
