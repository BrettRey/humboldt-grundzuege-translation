**Review status:** English/editorial review pending. This is a model-assisted research translation, not independently certified German fidelity.

For a time I wavered between two plans: publishing the individual grammars and dictionaries first, or first presenting the general conclusions drawn from the research into the individual languages. The latter course has the disadvantage that the reader can check the correctness of the general claims only very incompletely: resources for studying the individual languages must first be created for them, given the rarity and often very inconvenient arrangement of those that exist. But I was deterred from the former course by the protracted nature of the undertaking and the length of time that, given the difficulty of publishing such works, would necessarily have elapsed in the process.

## Translator’s notes

1. The two plans concern publication order: individual grammars and dictionaries first, or general conclusions first. “The latter course” refers to presenting the conclusions first; “the former course” refers to publishing the individual resources first. Presentation order does not establish the order of Humboldt’s research.

2. “Resources” follows the local use of Hülfsmittel in §3. “Those that exist” refers to existing resources, not languages. The colon makes explicit the explanatory connection expressed by the German relative clause. “Only very incompletely” concerns how fully the reader can check the claims; it does not imply total inability or faulty reasoning.

3. “Publishing” renders Herausgabe in both occurrences; editing or bringing out remains possible within its historical range. “Protracted nature” retains the lengthiness of the undertaking, without excluding wearisomeness. “Would necessarily have elapsed in the process” preserves the hypothetical, necessary delay of hätte … verfliessen müssen; it does not report time that actually elapsed.
