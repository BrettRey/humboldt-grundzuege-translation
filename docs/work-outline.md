## Basis and limits

English descriptions below are navigation aids, not accepted translations or a settled glossary. Section numbers refer to the 1906 edition. The text is unfinished. [D001](../DECISIONS.md) selects the printed reading provisionally; the report that Humboldt cancelled the first seven paragraphs remains unverified against manuscript evidence. This map describes the print, without reconstructing an authorially final arrangement.

## Argument landmarks

- **§§1–7:** the American-language programme and its justification. The opening distinguishes geographical grouping, language differences, external immigration and internal contact. The evidence note supplies the closer reading of §§1–2 and 7.
- **§§19–20, p. 374:** the general account includes language’s effects on thinking and feeling, and its movement from mind back to mind across individual, generation and nation. §20 rejects treating language merely as designation of thought formed independently of it. The paragraph continues beyond this sampled page.
- **§56, p. 400:** knowledge of the sound system is presented as foundational for grammatical investigation and understanding the language system. This is broader than a pronunciation inventory.
- **§72, p. 410:** the word is introduced through sound, concept and their unity; this section should inform later choices for *Wort*, *Begriff* and related terms. Only the opening on this page was checked here.
- **§127, p. 445:** the inquiry into combining words into discourse is distinguished from the usual philosophical grammar. Humboldt stresses the speaker and differences in grammatical approaches among peoples.
- **§149, concluding p. 473:** the surviving text ends while anticipating further investigation. Its closing passage allows degrees between grammatical indication and grammatical form, rather than a sharply cutting boundary.

These are sampled controls on future reading, not a continuous reconstruction of §§1–149.

## Surviving detailed contents

The detailed list begins at **§127**. Its footnote says that the beginning of this more detailed overview has not survived; this does not mean the main text of §§1–126 is missing.

For navigation, the listed topics cluster as follows: **127–130**, philosophical grammar, words and phrases, division and construction; **131–136**, historical/philosophical elements, logic and grammar, grammatical relations, language-form and thought-form; **137–141**, grammatical form, worldview and discourse-form; **142–149**, tacit/explicit grammar, mental supplementation, and grammar inherent in a language versus introduced into it. These clusters are mine, based on the contents, rather than new Humboldt headings.
