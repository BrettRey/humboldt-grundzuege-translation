## Argument map in nine stages

1. **Change the unit and standpoint of inquiry (445–448; §§127–130).** On 445 the subject is how humans connect given words into speech and embody unity of thought in unity of speech. This overlaps grammar but has a wider, more subjective standpoint than logical dissection of the grammatical whole. The assumption that words and expressions are already distinct is questioned. On 446 a whole thought is originally thought and uttered as one; isolated words can function as incomplete expressions, and whole expressions can be reproduced as single units. Grammar becomes necessary through reuse of words in different combinations. On 447 formulaic Chinese expressions support this point, and the methodological reversal is explicit: analyze speech into words, rather than assume composition from words. American-language examples show incomplete separation of what analysis would distinguish. On 448 Qquichua grouping and Tagalog linking illustrate departure from a whole; §130 then reintroduces variable recombination and the marking of connection. This account is Humboldt's proposed natural order, not an independently established account of acquisition.

2. **Constructional whole first, with historical and philosophical controls (449–450; §§130–132).** On 449 the constructional Typus must precede derivation of the characteristics of individual parts of speech. An underlying common organization guides grammar, yet abstract universal laws must not be imposed where historically specific investigation is required. A people's speaking and thinking are reciprocally related in this account. On 450 the explanatory ground is the whole living individuality of a people, studied through its linguistic product. Language also exists independently of each current speaker, and grammatical features can pass between peoples. Historical investigation is therefore essential, while clear philosophical concepts remain indispensable. Confusing concepts with facts turns an incomplete induction into a false necessity. §132 names language's nature, thought-laws, and vocal organs as sources of commonality, only the first two admitting purely conceptual derivation.

3. **Lawfulness enables freedom; grammar exceeds impersonal logic (451–453; §§132–133).** On 451 language is natural yet spiritually free, so its organization cannot be derived from bodily organism as such. Grammatical lawfulness secures free thought and enables freedom of period construction. Thought-laws provide grammar's necessary philosophical determinations. On 452 logic and general grammar overlap only in a delimited way: grammar adds existential positing, an active predicating subject, and reciprocal I/you. The author distinguishes necessary conceptual deductions from forms requiring an additional factual concept: cases, moods, voices, and number provide examples. On 453 the universal shared organization makes every grammatical form somehow recoverable in every language, even without a special designation. This is immediately qualified: the expression may approach from a different side and even destroy the proper meaning. Shared thought-laws do not entail identical grammatical views. Chinese lack of part-of-speech marks is not absence of the relations needed for understanding.

4. **Define grammatical view before phonetic technique; distinguish form from form (454–455; §§134–136).** On 454 grammatical variation has three levels: ideal view of relations, technical means of expressing them, and actual sounds. A grammatical relation concerns a word with respect to speech-connection. Gender is strictly lexical in its initial assignment but becomes grammatical through its effects on connection, especially when marked on a verb. Abstracting a constructional organization from particular utterances yields a language's grammatical view; it entails word classes and a regulated constructional pattern. On 455 that pattern admits some connections and excludes others. The central difficulty is separating the form of thinking from the form peculiar to language, not simply form from matter. Form is expressly law, direction, and procedure. The two forms are united in actual language; language's own contribution involves creative imagination and the symbolic embodiment of thought. The scan confirms this anchor.

5. **Satz and Periode have different principles of unity (456–458; §§136–138).** On 456 a grammatical whole depends on connected relations of governance. Thought-form tends to the unity of a simple or compound Satz; language-form to the Periode, adding the requirements of intelligibility, utterance, sensible embodiment, and a particular regulated organ. Their union is grammar's whole. On 457 the strongest language-form leads understanding by grammatical form itself, with lexical meaning supporting it. A verb viewed as an action-label differs from a verb viewed as the connecting means of a Satz. Guarani and Qquichua plural examples distinguish enough indication for thought and understanding from a systematic formal correspondence. On 458 restrictions of plural/gender by natural properties introduce worldly considerations, whereas arbitrary grammatical extension makes a distinction language's own. Accusative-infinitive and absolute participial constructions are language-specific: logically explainable once present, not deducible from the inventory of thought-functions.

6. **Three contributing principles and the productive unity of speech-form (459–461; §§139–141).** On 459 thought-laws, intuition of the world, and language's own form are distinct contributors. Abstracting content from periods reveals grammatical arrangement; exhaustive enumeration cannot supply its totality. On 460 creative imagination grasps the range of possible arrangements under linguistic constraints. Grammatical Redeform serves intelligibility, accompanies thought's articulated movements, and stimulates further thought. Pure language-form is analogous to artistic form: thought itself becomes its material. On 461 an unconscious Typus of grammatical joining completes that analogy. Greek, Sanskrit, Latin, newer related languages, Chinese, and other languages are evaluated by the extent of language-form's operation. Chinese is both the extreme contrast and a systematic achievement: it seems to gain pure intellectuality where it surrenders grammatical richness. The immediate purpose is conceptual discrimination, not a full comparative ranking by all three §134 levels.

7. **Locate grammar in the speaker or in the language; test mental supplementation (462–465; §§142–145).** On 462 the grammar operative as Typus or Sprachgesetz in the speaker must be distinguished from grammar in language itself. Language is both inward function and outwardly confronting material. The distinction is reformulated as tacit versus explicit grammar. On 463 an indifferent valuation of Chinese and Sanskrit is posed as a question to be resolved, not assumed. Missing copulas, unmarked case, and substantive expressions standing for relations illustrate Hinzudenken: supplying a connection or determination in thought. On 464 this includes time and existential positing; the Chinese example's original item is called an Ortsadverbium in the OCR and should not be silently modernized. A proper grammatical sign introduces no additional Sachbegriff. Yet mere designation cannot explain everything, since explicitly marking being would require further marking without end. On 465 the unified verbal form arrests this regress by presenting a verb as a whole. Its main force is awakening the distinction of form and matter. §145 concedes that word order, sense, and substantive relation-expressions can replace its aid to understanding.

8. **Separate comprehensibility from the larger claims for formal development (466–469; §§145–147).** On 466 replacement strategies leave the speaker using grammatical laws case by case, rather than experiencing form separated from matter. Humboldt attributes intellectual and expressive benefits to genuine grammatical form; these are his evaluative and causal claims, not findings established by this reading. On 467 individually tiny expressive losses are said to accumulate. The general deficiency is specified as failure to bring language-form to pure and complete Anschauung; the form remains operative as an Urtypus in every language. Unconditional domination of the formative faculty by language is expressly rejected. On 468 reciprocal production and the historically external force of language replace that one-way view. Many practical and scientific objects may suffer no significant disturbance, whereas philosophy, poetry, and style are said to expose the limitation or elicit compensating strengths. On 469 everyday intellectual individuality extends the claim. §147 acknowledges the strong objection: a form absent from overt designation can nevertheless guide speakers' understanding. The ensuing criterion deliberately isolates audible language from that apprehension.

9. **Make intrinsic and supplied grammar a graded comparative distinction (470–473; §§147–149).** The fixed-word-order allowance begins on 469 and continues on 470: the order must be immovable and refer to a fixed point identifiable independently of position. Chinese is said not to satisfy that condition. Habitually verbal words can nevertheless function as verbs, and substantive words can become relational as their earlier sense is forgotten. Consequently, historical knowledge of a people's apprehension may be required to decide what is genuinely grammatical. On 471 §148 explicitly opposes grammar actually contained in language to grammar brought by speakers; conflating them would flatten comparison. Three broad questions concern complete formal expression, systematic avoidance, or inadequate pursuit of it. On 472 these are explicitly denied the status of fixed classes: forcing languages into classes misunderstands their living individuality. §149 asks how much each language contributes to recovering speech-connection. Comparative characterization can proceed independently of settling the asserted effects on mind. On 473 the tacit/explicit distinction is upheld but has no sharply cutting boundary; grammatical indication approaches genuine form through indefinitely many degrees.

| **Typus / Construction / Constructionstypus** | An organizing pattern of speech construction, governing permissible connections and the treatment of parts; also the inwardly operative pattern, culminating in Urtypus. Artist's type and mathematical constructions provide local analogies. | Avoid a default taxonomic “type of language.” Construction concerns joining and arrangement; the mathematical occurrence on 460 is a different local application. Preserve whole-to-parts priority (449, scan checked). |
| **Rede / Redeverbindung / Redefügung** | Connected speech and its joining, encompassing a thought expressed as a whole. These concern activity and organized expression as well as its product. | Do not force Rede throughout into “sentence,” or treat Redeverbindung as just phonetic concatenation. |
| **Redensart / Wort** | An expression can be apprehended and reused as one, although analyzable into words. | **Satz / Periode** | Satz has unity through relations of thought; Periode incorporates these plus the conditions and resources of linguistic expression. A simple or compound Satz is explicitly allowed. | Keep a terminological distinction. “Period” may require explanation; do not assume it merely means a long sentence. |
| **Grammatik / Logik / allgemeine Grammatik** | They overlap in necessary conceptual determinations; grammar adds existential positing, active predication, and interpersonal I/you. | **Form / Sprachform / Denkform / Gedankenform** | Form means law, direction, mode of proceeding. Thought-form and language-form are distinguishable but synthetically fused in language. Do not make the analytically distinguished forms independently existing objects (455–456). |

## Source and interpretation cautions

- The checked §130, §136, and §148 anchors support a coherent sequence: constructional pattern first; distinguish thought-form from language-form; distinguish the speaker's grammatical contribution from what the language itself supplies. These are related distinctions, not interchangeable versions of one binary.
- Humboldt's hierarchy of linguistic perfection, national character, and claimed intellectual effects belongs to his argument. Report it accurately with its qualifications; do not present it as modern empirical consensus or quietly replace its vocabulary with modern theory.
- Conversely, the passage is not reducible to that hierarchy: it explicitly values Chinese systematicity and intellectuality, admits practical and scientific adequacy, rejects unconditional linguistic domination, requires historical investigation, disallows rigid classes, and ends with a graded boundary.
- Editorial footnotes on 448 and 459 report deleted wording; those deletions are not part of the current authorial exposition. References to other works on 447, 466, and 470 were read as apparatus only; their external contents were not consulted.
- The scan on 471 reads **Begriff** where the opening OCR reads **Begritf**. This correction is recorded, not silently entered into the evidence quotation. Other conspicuous OCR corruptions remain unresolved unless one of the three checked anchors covers them.
