## Argument in nine stages

1. **364–367: establish the American project and choose its mode of exposition.** Relative continental isolation and perceived broad resemblances justify considering American languages together. Humboldt explicitly rejects both their alleged uniform construction and their alleged radical difference from all other languages. He will begin with a comparative general account rather than delay publication until grammars and dictionaries are ready. This choice trades the integrity of each individual description for immediate comparison. The openly hierarchical judgments about alphabetic writing, literature, and national development belong to his argument and must remain visible as his judgments.
2. **367–370: separate two tasks, then qualify the natural-history analogy.** Languages can be considered in themselves or in geographical/historical connection; the tasks interact but differ in content and method (§7). No botanical-style language classification has yet succeeded. Lists of articles, genders, duals, and verbal devices do not disclose a language's whole nature: devices change while an effect or way of conceiving can persist. A language is an operation/mental process, so the analogy shifts from anatomy to physiology. Analysis into parts is necessary but subordinate to how the language attains its purpose in an individually modified manner.
3. **370–373: distinguish constituents from their coordinated procedure and justify presentation from the whole.** After studying means and constituents, one must investigate their use and combined treatment (§12 names the distinction with a warning against rigidity). Empirical study proceeds from individual languages and elements; exposition can begin from the general outline (§13). The whole's character is initially felt and only incompletely converted into analytic knowledge (§14). The ordering supports a comparative image of American languages before separate grammatical monographs (§15).
4. **373–376: construct the general point of comparison and connect language with thinking.** The general type is the type of the mental process, defined from the pure concept of language by abstracting from individualizing circumstances (§16). General grammar, insofar as it analyzes discourse and necessary word forms, covers only part of this task (§17). The projected outline (§18) includes language's return effects on thought and feeling across individuals and generations (§19). Language is the formative organ of thought (§20). Articulation is distinguished analytically from audible noise (§21), but the subsequent argument reasserts the essential role of sound, bodily organs, and hearing (§22). The claims about deaf people are historically situated arguments in this source, not an adequate present-day account of signed language.
5. **376–382: explain outward utterance, pleasure in speaking, and the need for another thinker.** Three motives converge in articulated sound (§23). Thinking moves outward and returns through hearing as an object (§§24–25). Animal cries lead to need, intention, and pleasure; human conversation adds development and communication of ideas (§§26–27). Mere useful cooperation cannot explain language's abundance. Sound has aesthetic and individuating importance (§§28–29). Sociability develops human powers (§30); an I needs a Thou and a response from another thinking power to complete conceptual objectivity (§31). Pronoun distinctions and inclusive/exclusive forms illustrate this, rather than supply a historical origin story. Communication both stimulates and tests thought (§32). Understanding, like speaking, activates one's own linguistic power (§33).
6. **382–386: nest individual powers and mark the speculative unity underlying their affinity.** Linguistic spheres run through human, group, national, dialectal, and irreducibly individual determinations (§33). Humboldt then proposes humanity as one nature divided into individuals, expressly in a tone of personal conviction (§34); its metaphysical basis is not claimed to be explainable (§35). His account of articulated understanding and children's growth of linguistic power supports that proposal (§36). Separation and mixture individuate nations and languages (§37), while common life can also produce dialect differences independently of descent (§38). His assertions about inherited organs and national affinities remain source claims, not findings certified by this reading.
7. **386–390: describe a language's world-view and its double relation to speakers.** In §39 a word mediates the object's impression, the subject's reception, and the word's own effect as linguistic sound. A distinctive world-view follows from their connected treatment across a language. Learning another language offers another standpoint, not a simple escape from all linguistic mediation. §40 then explicitly turns from speaking to language as an accumulating stock/system and relatively independent power. Dependence and independence are not to be divided into separate portions: language must be regenerated in thought, and is thereby made an object. Historical investigation begins in the middle of interacting inherited conditions (§41), where language strongly constrains an individual generation but remains modifiable (§42).
8. **390–395: replace misleading origin metaphors with gradual change and distinguish two kinds of likeness.** New languages emerge through separation and mixture, not historical invention (§43). Shared elements link languages that have been in contact (§44). This unity does not mean a single original language (§45); original-language and flood hypotheses do no explanatory work that Humboldt thinks warranted (§46). Resemblance produced through already formed language/contact differs conceptually from resemblance due to shared human capacities (§47); whether the two can reliably be separated empirically remains expressly open. The task is to recognize distinctive character within likeness, since shared power is itself individually determined (§48). Individuality extends through the whole language, from sounds to world-representation (§49), qualifying any sound-only reading of §29.
9. **396–399: place ultimate individuality in speakers, restore freedom, and divide the next inquiry.** National linguistic individuality is relative: true individuality lies in each speaker, and understanding includes a degree of non-understanding (§50). Individual power over language supplies the principle of freedom, whose bounds must still be investigated (§51). National change works through individual treatment and can include new, unanticipated ways of using language (§§52–53). Mechanical explanation and analysis are necessary but insufficient; living mind and inherited language must be examined in interaction (§54). §55 recapitulates act/material and natural connection/freedom, then distinguishes words from their combination in discourse as matter/form. It expressly rejects equating that division with sounds/rules. Sound must be treated first because both sides use it.

## Consequential distinctions and provisional rendering rules

- **Typus / Urtypus / Gattung / Art.** In §16, Typus belongs first to the common mental process, whose pure conceptual constraints supply a comparison point. Proposed default: **general type** or, where English demands clarification, **general pattern of the process**. Keep the German in a terminology note at first occurrence. It is neither an ancestral language nor an isolating/agglutinating classification here. Urtypus in §31 is the fundamental I/Thou relation expressed by pronouns: **fundamental pattern**, not proto-language. Art and Gattung sometimes support an explicit natural-history analogy, sometimes ordinary nested groupings (§§7, 33); do not force one taxonomic rank across all uses. Grundzüge denotes the intended main outlines, not a completed catalogue of types.
- **Sprache / Sprechen / Rede / Sprachkunde.** Use **language**, **speaking**, **speech/discourse**, and **study of language(s)** as initial defaults, preserving argument-specific shifts. Sprache may mean human language generally, one shared national language, a person's full linguistic capacity at a moment (§48), or the stock of words and system of rules (§40). §50 explicitly recognizes the legitimate narrower shared-forms sense while insisting on individualization. Sprechen stresses enactment; Rede can be the formed utterance/discourse analyzed into word forms (§17), so neither should uniformly become language. Sprachkunde names an inquiry or body of knowledge, not the faculty itself. **Linguistic science** is possible where the organized discipline is the bearer, but modern institutional connotations should be watched.
- **Bau / Verfahren / Mittel / Bestandtheile / Behandlung.** **Structure** is a workable default for Bau, provided it includes how parts work together rather than implying a static anatomical object. **Procedure / manner of operating** for Verfahren, **means/devices** for Mittel, **constituents/components** for Bestandtheile, and **treatment / handling / use** for Behandlung preserve the explicitly non-rigid distinction of §§10–12. Technical devices are not themselves the whole character; their presence is not identical to their effect (§369). Structural study remains necessary, so avoid translating the living-process insistence into hostility to grammatical detail (§29).
- **Form.** Keep a visible **form** family but annotate its bearer. Grammatical forms are technical devices (369, §17); fixed form can be a limitation that enables further thought (370); the form of mental activity is connected with articulation (§21); forms of language admit differently individualized use (§50). In §55 **form** specifically corresponds to **Redeverbindung**, versus words as matter. Translate the latter as **combination/linking in discourse** provisionally. Neither **syntax** nor a sounds-versus-rules gloss is adequate without qualification; the author expressly disallows the latter. Do not import a single later doctrine of inner form into these varied uses.
- **Bildung / Ausbildung / Entwicklung / bildsam.** Determine the bearer before selecting English. **Formation / forming** fits word and sentence formation, and the becoming of nations/languages (§§13, 43); **development / cultivation** fits powers and graded national/linguistic accomplishment (365, §§27, 32). Ausbildung can mark the fuller development of thought through interaction (§23), not schooling. Entwicklung and Wachsen are **development** and **growth** in the acquisition argument (§36); maintain their explicit rejection of mechanical word storage. Bildsamkeit (§42) is **plasticity / capacity to be shaped**, not Bildung with the same nominal gloss. The opening's evaluative developmental scale should not be erased by neutral-sounding terminology.
- **Eigenthümlichkeit / Individualität / Charakter.** Prefer **distinctiveness / distinctive character**, **individuality**, and **character**, respectively. Eigenthümlichkeit often names describable particularities; Individualität names the integrated singularity that analysis fails fully to capture (§14). They are closely related, not two non-overlapping technical substances. Most consequentially, the bearer shifts: the sound in §29 bears the language's true individuality; §49 insists on individuality throughout language; §50 locates true individuality only in the present speaker, national language being individual comparatively. Preserve the successive claims and their qualifications. Do not reduce them to a static group profile or assume that common patterns establish a modern theoretical ontology.
- **Geist / Gemüth / Seele / Kraft / Thätigkeit / Freiheit.** Use **mind** for Geist where it thinks, acts, and is affected; **mental/intellectual** for geistig in process descriptions; retain **spirit/spiritual** where the living-versus-dead or explicitly metaphysical emphasis would otherwise disappear (§§34–35, 54). Avoid choosing spiritual solely because the German is geistig. Seele and Gemüth occur as inward bearers of perception, feeling, reception, and linguistic response; provisional **soul / mind / inner life** must be settled sentence by sentence, without assuming identity with Geist or a modern faculty diagram. Kraft is a **power/capacity** capable of growth and exercise, and Thätigkeit is **activity**; keep them distinct from a given act and its product. Sprachkraft, Sprachvermögen, and Sprachfähigkeit are near-neighbours rather than certified strict synonyms; provisional **linguistic power**, **capacity for language**, **linguistic ability** preserves the variation. In §§51–54 freedom is contrasted with regular inherited determination; its effects are claimed to be irreducible to prior conditions but bounded. Neither random fluctuation nor arbitrary individual invention captures that claim.
- **Denken / Gedanke / Begriff / Vorstellung / Anschauung / Ansicht.** Use **thinking**, **thought**, **concept**, **representation**, **intuition/contemplation**, **view** as provisional differentiators, not an asserted Kantian terminological system. Thinking is an activity, a thought its formed content; a Vorstellung becomes a Begriff through the language-linked process (§20). §39 differentiates impression, subjective reception, and word effect within conceptual formation. Weltansicht is provisionally **view of the world / world-view**, not ideology or a fully articulated philosophical system. Preserve **world-representation** for Weltvorstellung (§49) and **way of viewing** for Anschauungsweise unless local reasons require otherwise. Ordinary Ansicht also means perspective, opinion, or side of the question (364, §§34, 40, 52), not always the language-specific Weltansicht. Translate foreseen Wirkung as **effect**, leaving stronger causal descriptions to sentences that actually warrant them.
- **Ton / Laut / articulirt / Articulation / Geräusch / Alphabet.** This omitted family is indispensable. The text analyzes articulated sound into articulation and audible noise (§21), then qualifies the abstraction by restoring physical air vibration and hearing (§22). **Sound**, **articulated sound**, **articulation**, **noise/audible sound** need local differentiation; mechanical **tone** for every Ton will mislead. Geräusch here can be the audible component, not merely unwanted noise. Lautsystem is a **sound system**; Alphabet can denote the human/language sound inventory (§§29, 33, 49), not always written letters. The argument uses historical deafness claims: do not silently update its conceptualization or translate them as contemporary claims about sign languages.
- **Gleichartigkeit / Einheit / Abstammung / Zusammenhang / Ursprachen.** **Similarity/likeness in kind**, **unity**, **descent**, **connection**, **original languages** preserve separate questions. §§43–45 constrain descent metaphors and distinguish shared/contact-modified elements from a single origin. §47 separates human-capacity likeness from historical transmission through already formed language, and explicitly leaves the available empirical means of separation undecided. Thus Einheit of human nature (§34), conceptual generality (§16), and historically connected languages (§45) cannot all be rendered as common ancestry. Read the metaphysical unity claim with its stated personal-conviction qualification.
- **Subject / Object / gegenständlich / Stoff / Erzeugniss.** Keep **subject/object**, **material**, and **product** visible enough to track the argument. A word becomes an object for its producer and for another thinker (§§25, 31); language becomes historically available material/power (§§40–42), but only exists in renewed thinking. Stoff is not simply a physical substance, and **dead** in §§54–55 contrasts inherited material with present animation; it does not mean merely an extinct language. These are reciprocally related aspects, not a product-versus-process choice imposed once for the whole work.
