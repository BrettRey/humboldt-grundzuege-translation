# Translation brief and terminology policy
<!-- SUMMARY: Concrete working brief inferred from the approved research-translation project and current preparation · status: current provisional brief · updated: 2026-11-15 -->

## Purpose and readers

Produce readable scholarly English through which a reader without German can follow Humboldt's argument and inspect consequential uncertainties. The intended product is a provisional research translation with separate commentary and source links. Brett's English approval concerns readability and editorial usefulness; it cannot authenticate German fidelity.

The project has no funding or assured specialist participation. Use held sources and existing tools. Model comparisons assist analysis but are not qualified human validation. No claim of a critical edition, definitive translation or publication readiness follows from this preparation.

## Source and textual layers

Follow the locally held GS V 1906 print under D001. Keep its reported cancellation of §§1–7 visible in opening drafts and exports. Keep source text, editorial apparatus, proposed grammatical analysis, English alternatives and commentary distinct. Do not silently merge a parallel fragment, a deleted reading or a later formulation into the base.

The full discovery index uses uncorrected OCR, with a labelled recovery for printed 410. Its matches locate possible contexts. Check the image before quoting a new passage or drafting its translation. Neither an index hit nor a second model's agreement upgrades an OCR reading to a checked quotation.

## English and terminology

Use the [individual v2 termbase](termbase.md) as the current conditional working reference under D009. The [earlier grouped register](terminology-register.md) is retained as history, not as evidence of complete terminology coverage. The [coverage report](v2/coverage-report.md) defines the completed inventory and its limits. For a consequential occurrence record: German form, bearer, local contrast, proposed English, source evidence and any exception. Reuse an English term when the same contrast continues; vary it when the source changes the bearer or relation. Preserve related vocabulary without forcing one English substitute per German lemma.

Preserve scope, modality, causal claims, significant repetition and metaphors. Long sentences may be divided when their relations remain recoverable. Do not add an unstated premise to make the argument easier. Keep historical hierarchies and problematic generalizations as the author's claims, with his actual qualifications; explanation and criticism belong in notes.

Notes should explain a consequential choice or limitation. They should not become a running substitute for the argument. Mark interpreter-specific vocabulary and later theoretical comparisons explicitly. Retain German at first explanation when an English label would otherwise collapse an important distinction, notably *Satz/Periode*.

## Basis and limits of this policy

Humboldt's 1816 translation introduction favors faithfulness to character, removes unsupported decoration, distinguishes the foreign element from mere strangeness, and separates translation from commentary (GS VIII 132–134; PRIMARY-A12–15). Its concern with Greek metre does not impose metre or unchanged German syntax on this prose project.

Nord's 2006 article supplies the practical question of purpose and readership and the option of documentary presentation with contextual explanation (targeted 131–135, 139–143; NORD). This brief adopts that orientation as a project inference; it does not assert Nord requires it for every scholarly translation. Neither source resolves D001's textual problem.
