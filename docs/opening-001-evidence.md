The opening explains why Humboldt groups the American languages geographically. It combines claims about the continent’s relative isolation with claims about contact among its peoples. The adjoining paragraph asserts similarities among their languages while rejecting their supposed identity. Those distinctions should remain visible in English.

There is also an unresolved editorial issue: **Drechsel reports that Humboldt crossed out the first seven paragraphs.** Our pilot translates §1 of Leitzmann’s 1906 printed text. Its status in Humboldt’s manuscript therefore needs checking before we present it as an accepted opening. The existing pilot is unchanged and remains unvalidated.

## E01. What is known about the opening’s status

Drechsel (2024:155; [chapter PDF p. 12](../sources/raw/references/drechsel-2024/drechsel-2024-12-3.pdf#page=12)) reports the cancellation while discussing *Grundzüge*. His printed statement was checked against the PDF image. This establishes what Drechsel says; I have not inspected the manuscript or the 2017 critical apparatus that could establish the marks, their extent, and their editorial interpretation.

Leitzmann’s [1906 editorial history, p. 481](../sources/raw/gs-v-1906.pdf#page=491), dates the work’s composition to 1824–1826 and describes it as unfinished. That entry does not confirm the cancellation. Neither does the opening source note or the transition from §7 to §8 in the pages checked. These limited negative checks do not refute Drechsel.

**Consequence:** retain the pilot as a study draft of the 1906 reading text and record the reported cancellation as unresolved. An edition policy can specify how that provisional text is labelled. Verifying the authorial marks requires the manuscript or a reliable critical apparatus; Brett’s assessment of the English cannot settle that evidence.

## E02. The closest primary parallel

The opening of [*Untersuchungen über die Amerikanischen Sprachen*, GS V, pp. 345–346](../sources/raw/gs-v-1906.pdf#page=355), closely matches the pilot passage. Its first page lacks a printed numeral; the following page is visibly numbered 346. Both images were checked. This is a separately titled fragment, not a second manuscript witness independently inspected here.

| Point of comparison | *Grundzüge*, pp. 364–365 | *Untersuchungen*, pp. 345–346 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initial justification | The continent’s position and its languages’ distinctive character justify the geographical grouping. | Substantially the same justification. |
| Sentence after the European, Asian and African grouping | *Auch zeigen die Sprachstämme eines jeden …* introduces differences of structure and attained *Bildung*. | *Grosse geschichtliche Begebenheiten haben die Völker und Sprachen in ihnen vermischt.* attributes mixture to historical events. |
| Immigration qualification | Explicitly says *neuesten Europäischen Einwanderungen* and *Nationaleigenthümlichkeit dieses Welttheils*. | Uses an elliptical reference to European immigration and later makes immigration from outside explicit. |
| Paragraph boundary | §1 ends with *einheimischen Verkehrs erfahren*. The discussion of linguistic similarity begins in §2. | §1 continues with claims about structural likeness, the limits of identity, and alphabetic writing. |

These differences explain why a translation assembled from both openings would misrepresent the chosen base text. They do not establish which wording came first or why Humboldt changed it. No direction of revision is inferred.

## E03. Constraints supplied by the surrounding argument

In [*Grundzüge* §2, p. 365](../sources/raw/gs-v-1906.pdf#page=375), Humboldt distinguishes *Aehnlichkeit* from an alleged *Einerleiheit* of linguistic structure. His opening’s geographical grouping should therefore preserve room for differences among the languages. In [§7, pp. 367–368](../sources/raw/gs-v-1906.pdf#page=377), he distinguishes studying languages within a general system from studying their geographical and historical connections. These are claims about his programme, not validation of his historical account.

The earlier [*Essai*, §§1–6, GS III, pp. 300–306](../sources/raw/references/humboldt-gs-03.pdf#page=312), supplies useful context. Its opening stresses American linguistic diversity and geographical barriers to communication. In §6, Humboldt treats available evidence about origins and relationships as inadequate and allows similar grammatical features to arise without transmission between peoples. A continental grouping alone consequently cannot warrant a claim of common descent. This last consequence is my inference from those passages.

Three constraints follow for the pilot:

- Keep external isolation and internal contact distinct. The paragraph expressly includes *Berührung unter einander*—contact among the peoples concerned.
- Preserve the uncertainty about earlier immigration and origins. Humboldt offers alternatives and limits his claim by the historical traces available to him.
- Preserve his evaluative vocabulary. The adjoining discussion of cultural development includes a hierarchy; a fluent translation should make the author’s position legible without endorsing it.

## E04. Terminology candidates, with their limits

These are working choices for a later comparison and audit. They are not accepted equivalents.

| German / current pilot choice | Evidence and consequence |
| --- | --- |
| *Grundzüge des allgemeinen Sprachtypus* / “Outlines of the General Type of Language” | Drechsel’s discussion at pp. 155–157 situates the title in a broader comparative programme. His English title uses “Fundamentals.” The opening alone does not settle “Outlines,” “Fundamentals,” or the scope of *Typus*. Keep the title provisional. |
| *Sprachkunde* / “linguistic scholarship” | §3, pp. 365–366, identifies a programme of investigating, describing and comparing languages. “Study of language” is another candidate. Consistency across the work needs checking. |
| *Sprachstämme* / “language stocks” | The grammatical subject here is linguistic; *Stämme, Horden und Familien* later describes human groups. Keep those referents distinct. This reading does not certify any modern genealogical classification. |
| *Bildung* / “development” | The local phrase concerns stages attained by languages. Underhill (2009:65, 74) discusses formation and cultivation in Humboldt’s wider usage. That context warns against imposing one English equivalent on every occurrence; it does not settle this sentence. |
| *innere Nationaleigenthümlichkeit* / “inner national character” | “National distinctiveness” is an alternative to examine. Manchester (1985:83–90, 103–106) distinguishes several uses of form and character. His interpretation is a reason to check the terminology, not proof that this occurrence names a particular theoretical construct. |
| *Gährung ihrer Kräfte* / “their powers were set fermenting” | Fermentation is present in the German. The parallel fragment retains it. A later audit should assess the metaphor’s English form and the sentence’s agency separately. |
| *des auf sich selbst beschränkten einheimischen Verkehrs* / “self-contained interaction among the native populations” | The paragraph links this with contact among the peoples themselves. “Contact within the continent” helps explain the reading, but is not yet an adopted replacement. It must not imply that each community lived in isolation. |
| *abschleifender Einfluss* / “smoothing effect” | “Levelling effect” remains a candidate. The following discussion of similarities supports a reading involving reduced differences; the precise strength of the English term remains interpretive. |
